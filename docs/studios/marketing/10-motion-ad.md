# Motion Ad Studio

The produced depth of video: a multi-scene ad with a storyboard you approve first —
several people across beats, each with their own lines, cut into one consistent film.

For one creator talking to camera in minutes, use UGC Ad Factory instead. That is the
quick path; this is the produced one. Testimonial and founder-story intents live in
UGC — this studio starts where a single to-camera cut stops.

## The Explainer Ads path

The Explainer Ads tile opens this same studio with the structure locked to
explainer and narration-first defaults: question hook → context → payoff → brand
tie, voiceover-led with captions on, no talking heads required. Built for cold
audiences — open on a question people actually wonder about, build the answer over
b-roll, land the brand as the natural conclusion. Everything below applies
unchanged; only the structure picker is hidden.

## How it works

1. Brief + product — a line about the ad, plus the product page link, photos, or
   description. Ingested facts ground the ad so benefits are never invented.
2. Style + cast + locations — pick the treatment, the people, and where it happens.
3. Storyboard — beat cards with a still, the cast, and the script line. Edit any
   beat, add beats in plain English, then approve.
4. Render — every beat renders in parallel with live progress, then stitches into
   one cut with music, voiceover, and captions.
5. Review — watch the assembled player with its quality score, re-render weak beats,
   and make more variants.

Nothing renders until you approve the board. Approval is the gate between planning
(free to redo) and rendering (costs money).

## Styles you can pick

The list follows your product's category, so food sees food treatments and fashion
sees fashion ones:

Food-forward (macro sensory, voiceover-led) · Dine-in social (food heroes cut with
real reactions) · Tutorial / demo · Before & after · Get-ready-with-me · Hero
cinematic · Feature demo · Lifestyle · Unboxing · Problem → solution → proof ·
Lookbook · Street style · Try-on haul · Luxury hero · Detail macro · Gifting moment.

Leave the brief empty and the director decides; write a line and it steers. Saved
style profiles let you train the brand look once — colours, grade, feel — and reuse
it on every later ad so campaigns match.

## Cast and locations

Three cast modes:

- **People** — describe each person (background, age range, gender). Generated for
  the ad, warm and relatable, never models — and never minors.
- **Group** — couple, small group, large group, or crowd for social scenes.
- **Product Hero** — no people at all; the product carries every beat.

Or bring your own cast from the avatar library: chosen faces bind to the on-camera
roles with reference photos, and each person stays identical across every beat
instead of drifting between renders. Locations work the same way — browse stock or
generate a setting, and it rides along as the scene reference.

## The storyboard

Each beat card shows its still, its camera, its cast, and its script line, with a
style-lock badge proving it shares the ad's seed, palette, and grade — one film,
not strangers cut together. Beats also carry their story role (hook, problem,
solution, proof, result, call-to-action) so the ad argues instead of wandering.

From the board you can:

- Edit any beat's visual or line in place.
- **Add a beat** in plain English at any position, with cast pinned or left to the
  director.
- Re-render a single beat without touching the rest.
- Re-stitch existing clips when only the assembly needs another pass.

If two neighbouring beats change worlds, the studio drafts a connector beat and
asks you to confirm it before planning continues.

## Render and takes

Beats render in parallel with per-beat progress. Before anything billable runs on a
re-take, the studio shows a cost confirm — no surprise charges. A beat can produce
several takes; you pick the one that lands and only the winner joins the cut.

Voice, music, and captions are pickers: who reads the voiceover, a music mood (or
your own soundtrack for consistency across a campaign), and caption looks including
TikTok word-pop styles.

Every ad closes with the standard end card — logo, your line, a scannable code with
your link — or your own end-card image. Sizes cover portrait, square, and
landscape with safe zones per channel.

## Score and variants

The finished cut carries a quality score with what to fix. Re-render the weakest
beat, or ask for more niches — the same board re-cast for parallel audiences.

## Your past ads

Ads autosave to history. Reopening one restores the brief, product, style, cast,
locations, board with your edits, takes, and the assembled cut.

## What uses credits

- Brief, style, cast, and location picks are free, as is the storyboard until you
  approve it.
- Rendering beats is the main cost — more beats and longer durations cost more.
- Re-rendering one beat or picking takes costs per beat, not per ad.
- Score, captions, music, and downloads are free beyond the render itself.
