# Comic Strip Studio

A short comic that explains, entertains, or sells — from your story idea to drawn
panels with speech bubbles, in one locked art style.

Three steps on screen: Setup → Storyboard → Render. The middle step is the point:
you approve and edit the plan before anything gets drawn, so you never pay to render
a story you haven't read.

## How it works

1. Setup — topic, cast, panel count, art style, and mode.
2. Storyboard — the story and per-panel scenes plus every speech line, all editable.
   Add, remove, or move bubbles; rewrite any scene.
3. Render — the drawn strip, plus an optional motion-comic video and post copy.

You can also start from the news: pick a tone and the studio proposes story ideas
from what's happening, then your pick flows into the same three steps.

## Setup choices

- **Panels** — 2, 3, 4, or 6. The grid lays itself out (2 in a row, 4 as 2×2,
  6 as 2×3).
- **Art styles** — Auto (the writer picks what fits the tone), Vector, Webcomic,
  Retro, Kawaii, Collage (scrapbook photo-cutout feel), Brutalist, Claymation,
  Glass 3D, Y2K, or Kinetic type-as-hero. Each style shows a sample tile so you pick
  with your eyes.
- **Modes** — Comic for invented stories, News explainer for real people (upload
  reference photos so likenesses stay right).
- **Cast** — name the characters once; the story and art stay consistent with them.

## Storyboard editing

Every panel shows its scene (what's happening — action, expression, setting) and its
bubbles (text, left or right, speech or thought). Edit any of it. Panels you didn't
touch reuse their art on re-render; only changed scenes redraw.

Two text-only updates never redraw anything: fixing dialogue, or re-rendering for
the motion-comic video.

## What you get

- The drawn strip as a single downloadable image.
- An optional **motion-comic MP4** — each panel shown full-frame in turn with slow
  pans and crossfades, 2–6 seconds per panel, with an optional music bed. Off by
  default; adds render time.
- Publish-ready post copy with hashtags for the finished comic.

## Your past comics

Comics autosave to history. Reopening one restores the topic, cast, style, panels,
storyboard edits, and rendered strip and video.

## What uses credits

- Topic, cast, style picks, and storyboard edits are free.
- Planning the story is a small amount.
- Drawing the strip is the main cost.
- The motion-comic video is an extra charge on top.
- Post copy and downloads are free.
