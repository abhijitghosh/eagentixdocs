# UGC Ad Factory

One creator talking to camera, fast and lo-fi, ready to post in minutes.

If you need a multi-scene ad with several people, locations, and a storyboard you approve
first, use Motion Ad Studio instead. This studio is the quick path: a single presenter,
a single product, one straight-to-camera cut.

## How it works

1. Tell it what you are advertising — a product page link, a short description, or photos.
   Any one of these is enough.
2. Pick a format and a presenter, plus tone and length settings.
3. Get a script built as short beats: what you see, what is said, and any text on screen.
   The hook always lands in the first 3 seconds.
4. Render the video: the presenter performs it in a handheld smartphone style, with
   voiceover, captions, and an end card with your logo and link.
5. Review and approve before you export or publish.

Review-style formats end with a soft recommendation ("worth trying if…"), never a
hard-sell shout.

## The script

- Runs 15–45 seconds, broken into short beats you can read and edit.
- Every script follows the same arc: hook → the problem → the solution → proof →
  result → what to do next.
- Some beats show the presenter speaking; others show the product or action while the
  same voice continues over the top.
- Hooks can pair a sound with the picture — for example a gasp or a snap before the
  first line — and on-screen text can add context, state the benefit, or carry proof.
- Anything the script claims must come from facts you supplied (product details, proof
  points, allowed claims). Anything you mark off-limits is never used, and never
  inflated into a stronger claim.

## The video

- Filmed-look options: Handheld (default phone-style shake), Static (phone propped up),
  First-person POV, or Studio (steady broadcast look).
- Setting, camera movement, colour look, and lens feel are pickers — the whole cut stays
  consistent with what you chose.
- Opening levers for the first frame and first line (visual surprise, whispered or
  direct-address read), plus an emotion the presenter performs across the cut.
- Captions are burned in with platform typography; the default style highlights each
  spoken word as it lands. You can turn them off.
- Optional music bed under the voiceover.
- Every video closes with the same standard end card: your logo, your closing line, a
  scannable code with your link, and an optional address. The main video stays clean.

Two render paths:

- **Standard** — clips per beat with the voiceover laid over the top.
- **True lip-sync** — one continuous take where the mouth matches the voice. Framing
  stays locked on the face, so camera movement is ignored on this path.

Saving money on re-renders:

- **Fix one beat, not the whole ad** — re-render only the beats you pick; the rest is
  reused, so a fix costs a fraction of a full render.
- **Draft vs Final** — draft renders at the cheapest quality for trying out script,
  casting, and movement; final renders at export quality.

## Your ads and versions

- Every ad is saved to history under a name you choose when you generate.
- The list shows one row per ad (always the newest cut). Opening an older version from
  the version dropdown plays that version — a bad new render never overwrites a good
  old one.
- Saving without rendering just updates the draft in place. Only a render creates a
  new version.
- Deleting an ad deletes it and all its versions.
- You can also rebuild a proven winner around a new product, hook, or format without
  starting from scratch, and you can paste in your own script and have it shaped into
  timed beats automatically.

## What uses credits

- Browsing formats, checking a script, and opening history are free.
- Writing or reshaping a script uses a small amount.
- Rendering video is the main cost; final quality costs more than draft, and fixing a
  single beat costs less than re-rendering the whole ad.
